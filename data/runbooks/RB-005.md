# OOMKilled pods: questions we get asked

Maintained by team-platform. Written for product engineers who are on call for their own service and want to know whether a restart loop is their bug or ours.

### Q: My pod restarted and the reason says OOMKilled. What actually happened?

Your container used more memory than its `resources.limits.memory`. The kernel's OOM killer picked a process in the container's cgroup and sent it SIGKILL. There is no warning and no chance to clean up. Kubernetes records it on the container status as `Reason: OOMKilled` with `Exit Code: 137`.

Check it with:

```
kubectl -n <namespace> describe pod <pod>
```

Look under `Last State: Terminated`. For a single field:

```
kubectl -n <namespace> get pod <pod> -o jsonpath='{.status.containerStatuses[0].lastState.terminated.reason}'
```

### Q: Why 137?

128 plus the signal number, and SIGKILL is 9. Any process killed by SIGKILL exits with 137, so the number alone does not prove an OOM. A container that ignored SIGTERM and was killed at the end of its grace period also shows 137, but its reason is `Error`, not `OOMKilled`. Always read the reason.

### Q: The pod status says Evicted, not OOMKilled. Same thing?

No. Eviction is the kubelet acting on node-level memory pressure, not your container passing its own limit. The pod is marked `Failed` with reason `Evicted` and a message such as "The node was low on resource: memory." Pods using more than they requested go first, ordered by priority and then by how far they are over their request. If you see evictions across several teams' pods on one node, it's a platform problem; ping #platform-oncall.

### Q: How do I tell a leak from a limit that's just too small?

Look at memory over a day, not at the moment of the kill.

- **Climbs steadily, then dies, then repeats:** leak. See the language-specific runbooks (orders-api for Node, fraud-scorer for Python, ledger-service for the JVM).
- **Flat until a traffic peak or a specific job, then dies:** the limit is too small for peak load. Raise it.
- **Dies within seconds of starting:** the process needs more memory at startup than the limit allows (large caches loaded at boot, model files). Raise the limit, or load lazily.

`kubectl top pod --containers -n <namespace>` gives a live view. Use the Grafana "Container memory (working set)" panel for history.

### Q: What does a real one look like?

In February, catalog-api-02.prod.internal was OOMKilled roughly every 50 minutes. Its memory graph climbed in a straight line. The cause was a response cache keyed by full query string, with no size cap. Separately, notification-worker-01.prod.internal was OOMKilled only during the Monday-morning email batch. The graph was flat all week, and the limit was simply below the batch's peak.

### Q: The runtime has its own memory limit. Which one wins?

Whichever is hit first. If the runtime's limit (Node `--max-old-space-size`, JVM `-Xmx` or `MaxRAMPercentage`) is above the container limit, you get OOMKilled with no stack trace. Set the runtime limit to about 75% of the container limit so the runtime fails first, with a useful error.

### Q: Can I just remove the memory limit?

No. Without a limit, one leaking pod can push the whole node into memory pressure and get *other* teams' pods evicted. Keep the limit; set the request close to it.

### Q: Who do I page?

Your own team for a single service. team-platform if the evictions are node-wide or many services are hit at once.

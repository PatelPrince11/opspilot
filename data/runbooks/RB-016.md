I'm writing this down after the May 2026 checkout incident, where we lost the first half hour looking at the wrong service. Checkout touches four hops, and each hop has its own idea of how long to wait and how often to try again. When the slowest hop gets slower, those ideas multiply instead of adding up.

Here is the path and the budget each layer has today:

```
client ──> edge-gateway ──> orders-api ──> payments-api ──> card-processor-proxy ──> card processor
           route timeout     3 attempts     3 attempts       mTLS egress, passes
           15 s              (PAYMENTS_     (CPX_MAX_        through the processor's
                             MAX_ATTEMPTS)  ATTEMPTS)        rate-limit replies
```

One checkout at the edge can become three calls from orders-api to payments-api, and each of those can become three calls from payments-api to the proxy. That's up to nine attempts against the processor for one button press. On a normal day nobody notices, because nearly every first attempt succeeds. The multiplier only shows up when the bottom of the stack is in trouble, which is exactly when you can least afford it.

The part I keep having to explain is that the timeouts don't line up. The edge gives up after 15 seconds and returns `504` to the client. But we don't propagate deadlines between orders-api and payments-api: nothing tells payments-api that the edge already stopped waiting. So payments-api keeps working through its attempts against card-processor-proxy for a request nobody will read. The client meanwhile often retries the checkout itself, which starts a fresh tree of up to nine attempts. Load on the processor keeps rising after the original trigger has gone away. That is the signature: **latency that keeps climbing after the cause is gone, plus more requests at the bottom than at the top.**

The trigger is usually the processor slowing down or throttling us. card-processor-proxy logs `CPX-W429` when the processor's rate limit pushes back. payments-api retries those, the extra attempts push us further over the limit, and more of them come back as `CPX-W429`. Once payments-api runs out of attempts it logs `PAYAPI-E1107` (card processor call failed after retries). orders-api sees the slow or failed calls and logs `ORD-5003`; its own retries add to the pile. Some calls just hang until a socket timeout and show up as `ETIMEDOUT` in the proxy logs.

How I tell this apart from an ordinary slow dependency, in about two minutes:

First, compare request rates per hop on the same dashboard: edge requests on the checkout route, orders-api calls to payments-api, payments-api calls to the proxy. In steady state the ratios sit close to 1:1:1. During a storm the lower hops run at two, three, or more times the edge rate. If the ratios are flat and everything is just slow, it's not a retry storm. Find the slow hop and treat it as a latency problem in that service.

Second, check whether `CPX-W429` and `PAYAPI-E1107` rise together on payments-api-01 through payments-api-04 and card-processor-proxy-01 and -02. If `CPX-W429` leads, the processor is pushing back and our retries are making it worse.

Third, look at the time order. If edge `504`s started first and the 429s only came later, our own retry traffic probably caused the 429s rather than responding to them.

What to do, in the order I'd do it:

- **Cut retries at one layer, not all of them.** Set `CPX_MAX_ATTEMPTS=1` in the payments-api ConfigMap and roll the Deployment. That removes the innermost multiplier, which is the one that hits the processor. Leave orders-api's attempts alone at first: one layer of retries is fine, stacked layers are the problem.
- **If the processor is still throttling us,** the remaining excess is real customer traffic. Talk to the payments on-call about shedding non-urgent payment calls; don't add replicas. More pods means more concurrent attempts against the same limit.
- **Watch the per-hop ratios come back to 1:1:1,** then watch processor latency recover. Restore `CPX_MAX_ATTEMPTS=3` only after the processor has been healthy for a while, and make it one change, not part of a bigger rollout.

Things I'd ask anyone not to do mid-incident: raise the edge route timeout (it just lets each tree run longer), or add a retry anywhere. Every retry you add is another multiplier.

The longer-term fixes are the boring ones: a retry budget per service instead of a fixed count, jittered backoff on every attempt, and passing the remaining deadline down the chain so payments-api stops when the edge has already given up. None of them are done yet.

For the record, the May 2026 incident followed this pattern exactly. The processor slowed down, `CPX-W429` climbed on card-processor-proxy, payments-api and orders-api multiplied the traffic, and checkout returned `504` at the edge for about forty minutes, with `PAYAPI-E1107` and `ORD-5003` throughout. It ended within minutes of setting `CPX_MAX_ATTEMPTS=1`.

# redis-cache is unreachable

redis-cache is Redis 7 with a primary and a replica (redis-cache-01.prod.internal, redis-cache-02.prod.internal), failed over by Sentinel. The real risk when it goes away isn't the cache. It's catalog-db: about 95% of catalog-api's product reads normally come from the cache, so without it every one of those reads goes to the database.

## Confirm

- [ ] catalog-api-01/02 logging `CAT-E301` (cache unavailable, reading through to catalog-db)?
- [ ] What do the errors say? `ETIMEDOUT` means the Redis VM is gone or unreachable (a dead host sends no reset). Connection refused means the host is up but the Redis process isn't listening.
- [ ] Ask Sentinel who the primary is. Sentinels run on redis-cache-02, redis-sessions-02 and internal-dns-01, port 26379:
  `redis-cli -h redis-cache-02.prod.internal -p 26379 SENTINEL get-master-addr-by-name cache`
- [ ] Can Sentinel fail over at all? `redis-cli -h redis-cache-02.prod.internal -p 26379 SENTINEL CKQUORUM cache`. With three Sentinels, the quorum and the majority are both 2. The quorum marks the primary down; the failover itself also needs a majority of the Sentinels to authorize it, so two of the three must be reachable.

## Expect

- [ ] An automatic failover takes about 40 s from the client's point of view: 30 s before Sentinel marks the primary down (`down-after-milliseconds 30000`), then election, promotion and clients finding the new primary. Clients see `ETIMEDOUT` until they've rediscovered it. Don't intervene inside that window.
- [ ] payments-api and inventory-service also use redis-cache, but only for reference data, with an in-process copy refreshed every 60 s. On Redis errors they log the error and keep serving their copy, so an outage shorter than a minute doesn't add load to their databases. Their Redis errors are noise for this checklist.

## Protect catalog-db

- [ ] Watch catalog-db-01.prod.internal CPU and the `CAT-E301` rate side by side.
- [ ] Connections won't run out. catalog-api's pools cap it at 2 × 15 = 30 sessions, so the pressure shows up as query volume and CPU, not as refused connections.
- [ ] If catalog-db CPU stays above 90% for more than a few minutes and failover hasn't happened, page team-catalog and team-platform.
- [ ] After the new primary is up, the cache starts empty or stale. Expect catalog-db load to stay high while hot keys refill, then fall as the hit ratio climbs back toward 95%.

## If Sentinel doesn't fail over

- [ ] `CKQUORUM` reports no majority: two of the three Sentinel hosts are down or partitioned. Restore a Sentinel (`sudo systemctl start redis-sentinel` on its host) and let Sentinel fail over.
- [ ] Sentinel has a majority but still doesn't fail over: page team-platform's lead.
- [ ] Don't promote a replica by hand.

## Done

- [ ] `SENTINEL get-master-addr-by-name cache` returns a reachable host, `CAT-E301` is at zero, and catalog-db CPU is back to its usual range.
- [ ] Ticket notes: what took the primary down, failover duration as clients saw it, peak catalog-db CPU.

Last time (December 2025), the redis-cache-01 VM was lost outright. Clients saw `ETIMEDOUT` for about 40 s until the failover to redis-cache-02 completed. catalog-api logged `CAT-E301` throughout, and catalog-db-01 CPU reached about 90%. payments-api and inventory-service logged Redis timeouts but served their in-process copies.

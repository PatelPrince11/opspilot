# Postmortem: reservations queries slowed by table bloat on inventory-db-01

sev2 · 2025-10-09 · 03:50 to 04:25 UTC · owner: team-commerce · runbook: RB-015

## Incident record

Checkout depends on a stock reservation, and from 03:50 to 04:25, which is 35 minutes, reservations took too long. inventory-service-02 logged `INV-E220` each time a reservation timed out, and orders-api logged `ORD-5003` against the same calls.

The database behind them, inventory-db-01, was answering, only slowly. Nothing recorded shows it refusing connections.

## Impact

Checkout reaches inventory through the reservation call, so checkouts that needed a reservation during the 35 minutes were slow or failed. The records hold no count of affected orders or customers, and the incident is rated sev2.

## What the slowness was

The `reservations` table is the inventory table that reservation queries read and write. A Postgres table that has gone through many updates and deletes carries leftover dead row versions until cleanup reclaims them. That extra material is table bloat: a query has to read past it, so the same lookup costs more pages than it should. On inventory-db-01 the reservations table had reached that condition, and reservation queries paid for it.

Its size, its dead-row count and whether a cleanup ran during the window were not captured, so no such figures appear here. What produced the bloat is not known either.

## Evidence on hand

The error logs are the main evidence. inventory-service-02 is the instance whose log is on record, and its `INV-E220` lines mark the reservations that timed out. Both codes began together at 03:50 and both had stopped by 04:25. RB-015 is the runbook for slow stock reservations and covers this path. No query plan, table statistics or session listing from the window made it into the record, which is why the explanation above rests on the table and the error codes alone.

## Reading the two codes together

The codes sit at two ends of one call. `INV-E220` comes from the inventory side and says a reservation timed out; `ORD-5003` comes from the orders side and says its call did not complete. Seeing both begin at 03:50 and end by 04:25 ties them to the same stretch of slow reservations rather than to two separate faults.

## Response

team-commerce took the incident, and by 04:25 reservations were completing again. Nothing is recorded about what was done to the table.

## Lessons

1. The first signal anyone has on record is the reservation timeout code, not a warning from the table itself.
2. The failure also surfaced on the orders side, a hop away from the table that caused it, so the inventory log was where the slow dependency showed.
3. Table size and dead-row counts taken at the time would show how large the bloat was.

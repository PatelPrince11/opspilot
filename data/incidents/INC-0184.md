# Tuesday 11 August 2026, the early hours: a feature store with no room left

`OOM command not allowed` was the reply redis-features-01 gave to a write from fraud-scorer-01 at 04:10, and fraud-scorer-02 received the same reply shortly after. Each scorer logged it under `FRD-E140`. This sev3 ran from 04:10 to 06:05 UTC, and the platform on-call had it throughout.

What that wording rules out is a node that is down. redis-features-01 was up and answering; it was declining to store anything new. The configuration explains why. Its `maxmemory` is 24gb and its policy is `noeviction`, so when the data hits that size Redis selects no keys to discard; every write is answered with the error until something changes. A store built never to forget keys acts this way once it fills. RB-019 covers this datastore.

The cost sits with the scorers. Scoring a payment bumps a counter for the card, and while writes fail the per-card figures drift away from real activity. The counters carry an expiry, so old values are not meant to linger forever, yet nothing new could be stored in the meantime. Only one machine hosts redis-features and nothing stands by for it, which ruled out shifting traffic elsewhere. The two scorer pods failing in one minute against the same node made the suspect obvious, and it was not either scorer.

The logs could not say what had filled the node, and which keys were responsible was left for later. What brought the errors to an end was not written down, so no sequence is given here. The outcome is recorded: both scorer pods stopped logging `FRD-E140`, and the incident closed at 06:05.

The whole thing lasted 115 minutes, from 04:10 to 06:05. A node set to refuse rather than evict goes on refusing while its memory stays full, and the early hour changed nothing about that. Nothing in the records says how full the node was beyond the fact that it sat at its ceiling, or how quickly it had got there, and no other host is involved: the two scorer pods and redis-features-01 are the whole picture.

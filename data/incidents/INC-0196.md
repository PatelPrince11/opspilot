# An evening of slow catalog reads, 15 September 2026

Tuesday 15 September 2026 was a sev3 for catalog-api. It started at 17:55 UTC and lasted until 22:40, which makes 285 minutes, or four hours and forty-five minutes. Both pods took part: catalog-api-01 and catalog-api-02 behaved alike.

Nothing was down. What people had was a service that answered, but late, and now and then a lookup that gave up and logged EAI_AGAIN, the resolver's temporary-failure status. Delay with the odd outright failure is what name resolution trouble looks like from the outside.

For context, catalog-api serves product reads with async workers and answers about 95% of them from the cache, falling back to catalog-db for the rest. Each of its two pods holds a database pool of 10 connections with room for 5 more. Those are the usual figures for the service, given here as background; the evening is not tied to any change in them.

The cause sits in how the pods resolve names. ndots:5 sends a two-dot name through the search list first, and catalog-api pods run with that cluster default: RB-021 notes that orders-api is alone in setting its own value, and catalog-api does not. A hostname with two dots, such as the one for its database, is therefore tried with the search suffixes before it is asked as written, which adds work to every lookup.

That fits both halves of the symptom. The extra work is the slowness, and when the retries of a question are all lost, the lookup fails with EAI_AGAIN. The details of the lookup call are not known: which resolver library catalog-api uses was not recorded, and so the number of questions behind one lookup is not stated here. RB-021 gives a figure for orders-api, which should not be carried over. Why replies went missing is a question RB-021 also leaves open, and no count of slow requests or EAI_AGAIN lines was kept.

The on-call engineer owned the incident, and the work between the first slow read at 17:55 and the end of the failures was not written up. Lines with EAI_AGAIN and lines about slow lookups appear in the logs of both catalog-api-01 and catalog-api-02. Both the slow responses and the lookup failures were gone by 22:40.

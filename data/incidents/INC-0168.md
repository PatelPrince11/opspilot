**Description.** Evening-only checkout slowness: the tail of `/checkout` jumps past 5 s for a minute or two at a time while the median stays normal. Orders go through; customers sit and wait. The database connection target is `orders-db-01.prod.internal`.

## Comments

**2026-06-16 09:10** · team-commerce, from alert history: on 15 June the checkout p99 alert opened and cleared five times between 19:30 and 22:00, never long enough to page. Same again last night. Put down to evening traffic.

**2026-06-17 20:40** · on-call, team-commerce: paged. Third night of the same shape.

**2026-06-17 20:55** · on-call: gateway logs for `/checkout` show every slow request finishing `200`, with nearly all of its time spent upstream in orders-api. Durations pile up a little past 5 s; a second, smaller pile sits a little past 10 s.

**2026-06-17 21:00** · platform on-call: walked RB-020 in case the database record was flapping in the `prod.internal` zone. `dig` against each internal-dns server gives identical answers and identical serials; coredns journals quiet. Ruled out.

**2026-06-17 21:10** · on-call: orders-api-01, -02 and -03 look healthy by every runtime measure: memory steady, no restarts, the event loop responsive. Something is waiting without blocking.

**2026-06-17 21:25** · on-call: from a pod, resolved the database hostname 200 times with `dns.lookup` and timed each. Nearly all answered within a few milliseconds; two needed a little over five seconds. The delay sits in name resolution.

**2026-06-17 21:40** · on-call: the pod resolver runs with `ndots:5`. Our database hostname contains only two dots, under that threshold, so glibc first tries it with each of the three cluster search suffixes and only then bare, asking for both A and AAAA records each time: 4 × 2 = 8 UDP queries per resolution. When one answer never arrives, the resolver sits out its 5 s timeout and asks again, usually with success. Two dropped answers within one resolution, on different queries, cost about 10 s while the request still succeeds. A query that loses both its attempts ends as `getaddrinfo EAI_AGAIN`: five such errors across the three nights, all for a hostname that resolves normally on demand. Why answers get dropped on this cluster is still unknown, and we are not guessing.

**2026-06-17 21:50** · on-call: the burst effect. orders-api's `pg` Pool closes connections idle longer than `idleTimeoutMillis`, left at the 10000 default. Between checkout bursts the pool empties, so each burst reconnects and each reconnect resolves the hostname again. Resolution runs on libuv's 4 worker threads, which file I/O also uses; a couple of resolutions stuck for 5 s leave the rest of the burst queued behind them.

**2026-06-17 22:15** · on-call: nothing safe to change tonight; stays sev3.

**2026-06-18 14:30** · team-commerce: reviewed change. (1) The orders-api Deployment's `dnsConfig` sets `ndots` to "2": the database hostname now goes out unsuffixed first, so one resolution is 2 queries rather than 8, a fourth as many chances to drop an answer. Shorter in-cluster names keep using the suffixes. (2) `idleTimeoutMillis` raised to 60000 so short lulls keep their connections. `UV_THREADPOOL_SIZE` stays as it is: extra worker threads would mask stalled lookups, not speed them up.

**2026-06-18 15:40** · team-commerce: rollout started.

**2026-06-18 16:00** · team-commerce: rollout complete on all three pods, which now resolve with `ndots:2`. Closing.

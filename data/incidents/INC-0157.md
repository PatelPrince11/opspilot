# Postmortem: payments-db-01 stopped when archiving failures filled its disk

Severity sev2. 2026-05-12, 02:20 to 05:10 UTC. Primary host: payments-db-01.prod.internal. Runbook used: RB-007. Prepared for the DBA review.

## Impact

payments-db-01 stopped serving. The data volume had no space left, the operating system reported `ENOSPC`, and Postgres shut itself down. Sessions that were connected at that moment were dropped with SQLSTATE `57P02`, the code that means the server killed them because another of its processes had crashed. With the primary gone, payments-api could not get database connections and its requests failed.

## What had gone wrong underneath

The cause was not a replication consumer and not a surge in writes. WAL archiving to object storage was failing. A WAL segment stays in `pg_wal` until the archive command has succeeded for it, so segments accumulated instead of being cleared, and the volume filled from the inside. This is the archiving branch of the payments-db WAL cases, as opposed to the stuck-slot branch.

We do not have a recorded reason for the upload failures. The review should establish what stopped the archive command working, because that is the real root cause behind this incident.

## How the night went

The incident was declared at 02:20 and closed at 05:10. The first job was to bring the primary back, which meant getting free space onto the volume and starting Postgres again. Only after the server was up did the team turn to the archiving fault, and once uploads succeeded the retained segments could be removed and disk use came down.

## Decisions and their trade-off

Service came first and understanding second. That order suited a stopped payments primary, since failed payments were piling up while the server was down, but it meant that the story of the archiving failure had to be reconstructed from logs afterwards instead of observed.

The sev2 rating reflects losing the primary of a payments service, not the size of the fault that led to it. A failing background job grew into a full outage.

## Customer-facing effect

Payment requests that depended on the database failed while the primary was down. The window of the incident, 02:20 to 05:10, is the outer bound for that effect; the ticket does not record when requests began succeeding again.

## What should change

- The alert on a rising archiver failure count exists to fire well before a volume is full. The review should ask why it did not prevent this stop, and repair whichever link failed.
- The ticket should carry peak disk usage, the failing archive step and the time archiving recovered.
- If the emergency space reserve on the volume was spent, restoring it is part of closing the incident.

# Q&A: the orders-api pool incident of 8 August 2026

sev2 · 10:15 to 10:40 UTC · cause: orders-db, seen in orders-api · runbook: none linked

**Where did it hurt?**

On orders-api-02 and orders-api-03. Each pod's `pg` Pool allows 25 connections; between them the pair could have 50 out at the same moment. Once those were all in use, requests that needed the database logged `ORD-5003` instead of being served.

**What was holding the connections?**

A slow query on orders-db. A pooled connection stays lent out while its query runs and comes back only with the answer, so while the query lasted, requests behind the busy connections queued. The query's identity and the cause of its slowness never made it into the record.

**Why only two pods?**

That is not known. The service runs more pods, but nothing was recorded about them for this window.

**Was the database out of connection slots?**

The arithmetic says it was not. orders-api runs three pods with 25 connections each, so its pools together reach 75 at most, and orders-db allows 300 connections. The trouble sat in how long each connection was held, not in how many could be opened.

**What ended the slow query?**

That was not recorded either, so the write-up stops at the moment the errors stopped.

**Were the pool settings touched?**

No change to pool settings is recorded for the window.

**How long did it last?**

From 10:15 to 10:40, which is 25 minutes. The on-call took it, and the errors were gone by 10:40.

**What did the logs show, in order?**

Pool usage on the two pods rose to its ceiling of 25 first. Only after that did requests start failing with `ORD-5003`, and the failures continued while the pools stayed full. Once the incident ended, usage returned to its ordinary range. The log lines are a sample of what the pods wrote, so they carry no failure count.

**What would have helped?**

A recorded name for the slow query. Without it, the same pattern on another day would start from the same blank.

**Open items**

- Find the query in the database's own statistics and write down why it ran long.
- Consider a gauge on pool usage that pages ahead of the first `ORD-5003`.

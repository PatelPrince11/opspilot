# The Sunday login flood

I was on call for team-identity on Sunday 3 August 2025. This is my account of a sev2 that ran from 10:10 to 10:45 UTC.

The page came at 10:14: the edge's `503` rate on `/v1/auth/login` was over its threshold. Other routes looked fine at first glance. The login graph didn't: attempts had gone from the usual 15 or so a second to about 150 a second at 10:08, and about 97% of them were failing on the password. They came from a wide spread of addresses. That pattern is credential stuffing, someone replaying leaked username and password pairs against us.

auth-service checks each password with bcrypt on the request goroutine. bcrypt is meant to be expensive, which is the point when someone steals the hashes and the problem when someone floods the login form. Each pod also caps itself: once 64 logins are in flight it answers `503` instead of queueing more, and the edge passed those straight through. The first ones went out at 10:10.

At 10:20 I worked through RB-002, because both pods were sitting exactly at their CPU limit. The throttling view showed about 70% of periods throttled, which on its own would send you looking for a thread count sized to the node. That wasn't it. Our Deployment sets `GOMAXPROCS` from the CPU limit, and it was 2 on both auth-service-01 and auth-service-02, matching the limit. This wasn't a mismatch making a half-idle pod stall; the pods really needed more CPU than the 2 they were allowed.

There is no rate limit on the login route at the edge; the only edge limit we have is on `/search`. So the quickest lever was the one RB-002 describes. At 10:27 I raised the CPU limit from 2 to 4 per pod and left the requests alone, so scheduling didn't change. The Deployment rolled one extra pod at a time with none taken away, and was done at 10:33. The `503`s stopped at 10:34, while the attempts were still arriving at the same rate. At 10:40 the attempts stopped as abruptly as they had started. I watched for five more minutes and closed it at 10:45.

What I'd like done:

1. A rate limit on `/v1/auth/login`, per client address and overall. It's not built, and nothing at the edge could have helped today.
2. An alert on the share of failed logins, which would have fired before the CPU did.
3. Keep the limit at 4 until the rate limit exists, then decide.
4. Accounts that saw a successful login during the run get a forced password reset. Security has the list.

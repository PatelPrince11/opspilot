# Night on call: payments that waited for a connection

I was the team-payments on-call on Wednesday 6 May 2026. This sev2 ran from 02:35 to 03:10 UTC, and this is how it looked from my seat.

The page was for the `503` rate on the payment routes. In the payments-api logs the line that mattered was `PAYAPI-E1042`: a request had waited the full 500ms `DB_ACQUIRE_TIMEOUT` for a pooled database connection and been turned away. Every pod holds up to 40 connections (`pool_max_conns=40`, four pods), and on payments-api-02 and payments-api-03 all of them were in use. The pods were not short of capacity in the usual sense; the connections were simply not coming back.

My first question was whether the trouble was local to one pod or shared. It showed on both payments-api-02 and payments-api-03, so I stopped suspecting a single stuck process and went to the database. I took a goroutine dump from payments-api-02 before touching anything, for the ticket.

On payments-db-01 the sessions belonging to the payments login were mostly busy running the same statement. The pods were waiting on slow statements, and those statements kept the pool occupied. I am not naming the statement here. I did not establish why it was slow, and I would rather the ticket say so than guess.

RB-010 is the runbook for this pool-exhaustion pattern, and its mitigation is to restart after taking the dump. I restarted the payments-api pods once the dump was saved, which released every held connection. The pools drained, `PAYAPI-E1042` stopped, and by 03:10 acquire wait was back to its healthy figure of under 50 ms. I closed the incident then.

A few things about the shape of the night. Nothing about the application itself needed to change, and the pool and timeout settings stayed as they were. The only intervention was the restart, which is a bandage: the slow statements, or whatever produced them, were not addressed by anything I did.

For the review:

- Why those statements were slow. A restart clears the symptom and leaves this question open.
- Whether to alert on acquired connections per pod, so that someone hears about a filling pool before payments start returning 503.
- Whether 500ms is the right deadline: it makes a slow database visible within a second, which is good for detection and hard on customers.

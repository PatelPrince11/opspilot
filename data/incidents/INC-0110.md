# Postmortem: card payments and the processor handshake, 19 December 2025

Severity sev1. All times UTC. Window: 21:37 to 22:47, which is 70 minutes. Runbook consulted: RB-023. Authors: team-payments.

## At a glance

On the evening of 19 December 2025 a CA rotation on the card processor's side broke the TLS handshake that card-processor-proxy makes towards the processor. `CPX-E600` marks a failed TLS handshake with the processor. From 21:37 each of the two proxy pods, card-processor-proxy-01 and -02, logged it on every outbound attempt, and payments-api returned `PAYAPI-E1107` once its 3 attempts on a payment were spent. For 70 minutes card payments could not be completed.

## Who saw what

Both pods began failing at the same minute, and the connection to the processor broke during setup each time. Identical behaviour on two independent pods pointed away from a single bad instance and away from a capacity problem, and towards something every connection to the processor had in common.

payments-api sits one step upstream. A payment that met the failing handshake on each of its attempts came back as failed. Retrying could not help, since an attempt through either pod met the same condition.

## Scope

The two proxy pods are the route from payments-api to the processor, so the failure sat on the one path that card authorisations take. The proxy is configured to make up to 3 attempts towards the processor as well, so a single payment could produce several failed handshakes before payments-api gave up on it. The rate of failed payments during the window was not recorded, so the size of the loss is not known.

## What it was not

The symptom resembles the one RB-023 covers for a lapsed client certificate of ours, and that resemblance made the runbook tempting to follow. It did not apply: our client certificate was not the problem, so a replacement from the processor was not the way out.

The rotation was on the processor's side; no detail about it is recorded. Which certificate or which verification failed is not recorded either.

## What the incident showed

A failure on the processor's side can look, from our logs, like a fault in our own TLS setup. The match with RB-023 is why a responder would first suspect our client certificate. Cause and fix for a processor-side rotation are not covered by that runbook, which is built for a lapse of our own certificate.

## How it ended

team-payments kept the incident, and card payments recovered by 22:47. The step that ended the failures was not recorded, and neither was the time at which anyone acted. No payment count for the window was kept.

## Still to settle

- How a handshake failure caused by the processor's side differs, in `CPX-E600`, from one caused by our own certificate, so the next responder can tell them apart early.
- Whether a CA rotation at the processor is announced ahead of time, and who on our side would receive that notice.

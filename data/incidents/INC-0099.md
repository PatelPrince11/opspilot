# redis-sessions failover on 12 November 2025: what happened

team-identity with team-platform. sev3. 18:05 to 18:15 UTC. We followed RB-018's checks during the incident.

**What did users see?**

From 18:05 some users were asked to log in again (`AUTH-E401X`). The sessions that were lost belong to logins handled by auth-service-02 between 18:05 and 18:07:45. A smaller group, whose sessions were created through the other auth-service instance in that window, also had a lookup fail when their next request went through auth-service-02; their sessions were not lost, and logging in again fixed it. By 18:09 `AUTH-E401X` was back to its usual trickle.

**Which node was the primary?**

redis-sessions-02, since the planned failover on 7 October 2025. redis-sessions-01 was patched after that one and stayed a replica. Since this incident redis-sessions-01 is the primary again.

**Why did Sentinel fail over?**

At 18:04:30 a host-firewall rule was added by hand on redis-sessions-02 during hardening work. It dropped traffic from the two VMs that run our other Sentinels, and nothing else: auth-service pods and the replication link from redis-sessions-01 still got through. Those two Sentinels stopped getting replies, waited out the 30 s `down-after-milliseconds`, agreed at 18:05:00 (2 of 3, our quorum), and promoted redis-sessions-01 at 18:05:05. Up to that moment replication was working, so nothing written before the failover was lost.

**Why were sessions lost after that?**

redis-sessions-02 never learned it had been replaced: the Sentinels that knew couldn't reach it. It still answered as a primary. The other auth-service instance switched to redis-sessions-01 at the failover; auth-service-02 kept its connections to redis-sessions-02 and went on writing sessions there. redis-sessions-01 had stopped replicating from it, so those writes existed only on the old node. The Redis Sentinel docs describe exactly this: writes accepted by an old master that clients can still reach are discarded when it rejoins as a replica.

**Where does `READONLY` come in?**

At 18:07:40 the rule was removed. The Sentinels reached redis-sessions-02 again and at 18:07:45 reconfigured it as a replica of redis-sessions-01, which drops its own data and copies the new primary's. auth-service-02 was still pointed at it, so its next session writes got `READONLY` until 18:07:49, when it asked Sentinel again and moved over. That four-second window only works because the demoted node was reachable.

**How did we confirm it was over?**

At 18:09 Sentinel named redis-sessions-01 as master, and `INFO replication` on redis-sessions-02 showed it as a replica (`role:slave`). At 18:15 the replica's offset had caught up with the primary's, and we closed it.

**What changes?**

- Host-firewall changes on the Redis VMs go through config management with a review, never by hand (team-platform).
- team-platform will look at `min-replicas-to-write` for the sessions primary. The Sentinel docs describe it as the way to stop a primary that has lost its replicas from accepting writes.
- No change to auth-service. Completing the login when the session write fails is still the right call; it is why only the next request failed and not the login itself.

# ledger-db-02 root disk: questions and answers

Monday 9 February 2026, 16:00 to 16:45 UTC, sev3, RB-008. Replies from the DBA lead to colleagues in finance engineering and platform.

**What tipped us off?**

A scripted probe on ledger-db-02.prod.internal stopped reporting at 16:00. Before it sends a result, it creates a scratch file; the operating system refused that file with `ENOSPC`. The resulting alert landed with the database rota.

**Did the ledger database stop serving?**

It kept going. From my laptop I issued a handful of read queries and every one came back. I'd rather not promise that every local write on that machine succeeded, because plainly some did not: the probe's scratch file is one example. My claim is narrower. Postgres logged no errors that we spotted.

**How does a database host fill up when its database hasn't grown?**

On our VMs the root filesystem also carries `/var`, and plenty besides Postgres keeps files there. `df -h /var` read 100%. A ranked listing of directories beneath it put `/var/log/journal` at the top by a huge margin.

**What made that directory balloon?**

Nothing ever limited it. `journalctl --disk-usage` matched the listing, and this machine's `/etc/systemd/journald.conf` contained no size directive. Left alone, journald retains everything it records.

**How was space recovered?**

With `journalctl --vacuum-size=500M`, which tells journald itself to discard its oldest archives until the total fits. Removing files from that directory manually is riskier, since journald keeps its own index of them. Headroom returned at 16:14.

**What keeps it from recurring here?**

At 16:21 the line `SystemMaxUse=500M` went into journald.conf, followed by a bounce of the journald service. A fresh size reading afterwards came in beneath that limit. The vacuum on its own would only have postponed a repeat.

**Where does 500M come from?**

It's our runbook's figure. Enough recent history survives for debugging, while the journal stops competing with everything else that shares this disk.

**Total elapsed time?**

45 minutes between the first failed probe and closure, mostly spent observing usage after the change to make sure it held steady.

**Any regrets?**

My opening minutes went on Postgres health, which is understandable on a ledger host but slower. Next time, `ENOSPC` from a probe on a database VM sends me to the root disk first.

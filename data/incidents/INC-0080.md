## Two restarts, one cause

RB-006 lists the restart as a stopgap, and this night showed what that word means. The 04:50 restart was the right call for the memory: fraud-scorer-02 stood at about 2,860 MiB of its 3,072 MiB limit at 04:38, and the restart pulled it back. But the cache inside each worker grows whenever reads from redis-features are slow, and at 05:02 the reads were still slow. Within 18 minutes of the new pods taking traffic the warnings were back and the curve was climbing again.

The second restart held because the store was already answering normally when it ran. Waiting for the store before refreshing the pods is the order to follow when the store is still misbehaving.

## Why the cause took two hours

The 04:22 latency check did show the problem, but it looked irregular, and the on-call did not trust it on its own. The scorer saw bursts of slow reads, not a wall, and bursts are easy to wave away at night. Only the second look at 05:48 made it clear the store was the thing to wait on. Starting on the payments side cost the first part of the night too.

## Why sev2

From 03:40 payments-api logged `PAYAPI-W0310` well above its night-time level, and the first restart did nothing about the slow store behind it.

## Carry-forward

- Treat irregular `--latency` spikes on redis-features as real when `FRD-W118` is high.
- Note the rss figure at each restart, so the next on-call can see how fast the cache refills.
- Keep the "fix the store, then restart" order in the team's notes for the next one.

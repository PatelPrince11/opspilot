# Late emails on 29 June 2026 (sev4)

Owner: team-growth. Window: 17:30 to 21:10 UTC. Runbook used: RB-009.

## What customers noticed

On the evening of Monday 29 June, order confirmations and shipping emails arrived late, the worst of them about an hour late. Every email was eventually sent. Nothing else in checkout or payments was involved.

## Timeline (UTC)

- 17:30 · the email provider's API starts answering slowly; consumer lag on `notifications.outbound` begins to climb.
- 17:52 · lag passes 20,000 messages and notification-worker-01 and notification-worker-02 both log `NTF-E710`.
- 18:05 · team-growth on-call picks it up. Both pods healthy, no restarts, sends succeeding, just slowly.
- 18:30 · used RB-009's retention check: `notifications.outbound` has no retention override, so it keeps the broker default of 168 h, while lag for the `notification-worker` group was about 54,000 messages. We were minutes behind, not days. No retention change, no disk work.
- 20:30 · the provider recovers; sends are back to their usual speed.
- 20:59 · lag drops below 20,000; `NTF-E710` stops.
- 21:03 · lag reaches zero.
- 21:10 · resolved.

## Why the backlog grew

Each worker pod keeps 20 sends in flight, so the two pods have 40 going at once. At the usual 0.33 s per send that is about 120 sends a second, three times the evening arrival rate of about 40 messages a second. During the slowdown a send took about 1.6 s, so throughput fell to 40 / 1.6 = 25 a second, 15 a second short of what was arriving.

At 15 a second the backlog reached the 20,000 threshold after about 22 minutes, at 17:52. By 18:30 it was 15 × 3,600 = 54,000, which at 40 a second is roughly 23 minutes of mail. By 20:30, three hours in, it stood at about 15 × 10,800 = 162,000, a little over an hour of mail.

Once sends were fast again, the workers ran at about 120 a second against 40 arriving, so the backlog shrank by about 80 a second. Getting from 162,000 down to 20,000 took about 1,775 s, roughly 29 minutes (20:59); emptying it took about 2,025 s (21:03).

## What we did not do

We didn't add pods or raise the in-flight count: the provider was the slow part, and pushing more concurrent requests at a degraded API rarely helps. We didn't touch retention: the oldest unread message was never close to the topic's limit.

## Follow-ups

1. Alert on lag measured in minutes of traffic, not a fixed message count; 20,000 means very different delays at noon and at night.
2. Ask the provider for their incident report.
3. Add a status line to the customer support dashboard when email is running late.

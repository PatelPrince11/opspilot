# Ticket: certificate error reported by one client, edge certificate in date

**Description.** A client reports that its device refuses the certificate our edge presents. No one else has reported anything similar.

## Comments

**13:40** · support: ticket opened from the client's report. The refusal happens during the connection itself, before any request is sent.

**13:55** · platform on-call: searched the edge logs from the time of the report. edge-gateway-03 has a handshake that the peer broke off at 13:41, which fits the report and may be the client trying again; nothing in the line says which client it was. New connections are spread evenly over all three edge pods, so another attempt from the same device could reach any of them.

**14:10** · platform on-call: read the certificate that edge-gateway-03 hands out. Its dates are current; it does not run out until 2025-03-16 10:15 UTC, about two months away. The chain is complete. Only this pod was checked. That was enough here, because the question was whether the device or the certificate was at fault, not which pod the device reached.

**14:25** · platform on-call: a certificate that is in date, refused by one device while no other client complains, points at that device. RB-025 lists this case: one client failing while everyone else is fine points at the client's clock, with nothing to change at the edge. A device sees a certificate as expired or not yet valid when its own date falls outside the certificate's dates.

**14:40** · platform on-call: answer sent to support, asking that the client check the date and time on the device.

**15:25** · platform on-call: closing as a client-side fault. No change to the edge.

## Why only edge-gateway-03 is named

The fault sits on the client, not on a pod. A device with a wrong clock refuses a valid certificate whichever pod answers, and the load balancer spreads new connections evenly over the three pods, so the pod named here is chance. edge-gateway-03 appears only because its log holds the broken-off handshake from 13:41 and because it was the pod whose certificate was read. It was not changed.

## Why this stayed at sev4

One client reported a problem, no other client did, and edge-gateway-03 kept serving requests normally throughout. The ticket was handled through support rather than as an outage, and it was open for 105 minutes (13:40 to 15:25).

## Left alone on purpose

- The Secret `edge-public-tls` was not touched. The certificate in it was valid.
- No pod was restarted and no rollout was started. A restart reloads the same file and would not change what the client's device decides.
- No early renewal. The certificate was valid; the fault was the device's clock.

## What this ticket does not record

It does not name the client, say what kind of device it used or quote the exact message the device showed. It also does not record whether the client confirmed the clock fix afterwards; the ticket was closed on the evidence above, not on a reply from the client.

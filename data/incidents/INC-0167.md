# The 13 June 2026 crawler burst on /search, as questions and answers

sev4 · 10:55 to 11:20 UTC · cause: edge-gateway limit, seen in search-api · runbook: RB-030

**What was seen?**

From 10:55 a share of `/search` requests came back from the edge as `429`, and the body of each carried `EDGE-RL01`. All three edge pods (-01, -02 and -03) logged it, so it was not one pod having a bad day.

**Who was affected?**

Anyone whose search reached a pod with an empty bucket got the refusal, crawler and ordinary users alike, because the limit does not tell them apart. Searches that arrived while a bucket had tokens were served as usual.

**Why did search-api look calm?**

The refused requests were stopped at the edge and never reached it, so search-api logged no errors of its own and saw only the traffic that was let through. Reading search-api alone would have shown a healthy service. RB-030 describes this signature from an earlier case.

**What was the limit?**

Each of the three edge pods keeps a local bucket for the `/search` route only, set to 250 requests per second per pod, which makes 3 x 250 = 750 per second across the three. When a pod's bucket is empty it answers at once and the request goes no further. Other routes have no such bucket.

**What was sending the traffic?**

A crawler. Its burst of requests to `/search` went past 250 per second on each pod. The crawler's operator, its address range and its request rate were not recorded.

**How long did it last?**

25 minutes, from 10:55 to 11:20, at sev4. Who dealt with it and what, if anything, was changed were not recorded; the refusals were gone by 11:20.

**What stayed unknown?**

How much of the refused traffic was the crawler and how much was ordinary search was not recorded, and neither was the share of refused requests.

**What would help next time?**

- Count `EDGE-RL01` responses per pod for `/search` and keep that count next to search-api's request rate.
- Decide who owns blocking a crawler at the edge, since the limit alone only caps it.

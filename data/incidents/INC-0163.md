Between 22:11 and 02:30 the pod was killed about 20 times, once every 10 to 15 minutes.

## The arithmetic

A warmed-up worker holds about 150 MiB. Sixteen workers need 16 × 150 = 2,400 MiB, inside the 3,072 MiB (3 GiB) limit. Thirty-two need 4,800 MiB. The kill came once about 20 workers had warmed up (3,072 / 150 ≈ 20), which is why each start lasted about 11 minutes.

## Why RB-006 didn't point here

RB-006 is about memory growing inside each worker. Its stopgap, the `--max-requests` recycling, bounds how big one worker gets, not how many there are. Every check in it came back clean, which in the end was the clue: per-worker memory was normal, there were just twice as many workers.

## Follow-ups

- Keep `WEB_CONCURRENCY` set explicitly (now 16); never rely on the CPU-count fallback.
- Check the other Deployments whose worker or thread count comes from the visible CPU count, now that the pool mixes 16- and 32-core nodes.

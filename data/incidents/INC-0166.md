# Ticket: 504 at the edge, orders-api-01 not answering

**Severity:** sev3
**Window:** 2026-06-09, 13:05 to 13:30 UTC
**Host:** orders-api-01.prod.internal
**Seen at:** edge-gateway
**Runbooks:** RB-014, RB-004
**State:** closed, errors stopped by 13:30

## What was reported

Slow checkout requests came back from the edge as `504`. The edge gives an upstream 15 seconds before it gives up, so each of these was a request that orders-api-01 never finished inside that time. The edge log is where the failures showed; the cause sat one hop behind it. RB-014 is the edge triage table that sends a `504` on `/checkout` to orders-api.

## What the host was doing

Node.js runs request handlers on one event loop. When a handler does synchronous crypto work, the loop is busy until that work ends, and no other handler in the process gets a turn, however many requests are waiting. From 13:05 to 13:30, which is 25 minutes, orders-api-01 was in that state. Requests queued behind the blocking work, and those that waited past the edge's limit came back as `504`, beginning within the first minute.

Seen from outside, a frozen loop looks like a slow dependency, because the waiting happens inside the process while its own calls are healthy. RB-004 describes that appearance in its memory-leak setting; it is cited here for the event-loop view only, and nothing in this ticket concerns the heap.

## Why one busy handler affects all the others

The event loop is a single thread that picks up work in turn: it reads a new request, runs a handler until the handler waits on something, resumes a handler whose reply has arrived, and writes responses back. Asynchronous calls to the database or to another service hand their waiting to the system, so the loop stays free. A synchronous crypto call does the opposite. It computes on that one thread and returns only when it finishes, and everything else in the process is held until then, including replies that have already arrived and responses that are ready to be written.

That is why the damage was not limited to the requests that triggered the crypto work. Checkouts on orders-api-01 during the window sat behind it, whichever customer they belonged to.

## Impact

Checkout requests served by orders-api-01 between 13:05 and 13:30 were slow, and those that waited the full 15 seconds failed at the edge. No count of failed checkouts or affected customers was kept.

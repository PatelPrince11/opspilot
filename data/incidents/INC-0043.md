# Postmortem: the edge's certificate file, 15 May 2025

Thursday 15 May 2025, 16:07 to 16:32 UTC, 25 minutes, sev1. Team: team-platform. Runbook: RB-025.

## Impact

For 25 minutes some of the people and programs that talk to our public edge could not open a TLS connection. The edge is three pods, edge-gateway-03, edge-gateway-01 and edge-gateway-02, and all three were serving the same incomplete certificate file. Which clients failed depended on what they already held: those without a stored copy of the intermediate could not finish the handshake, and the rest carried on. For a refused client the failure came before any HTTP was sent, so the routes behind the edge never saw its requests. That makes the damage uneven by nature, and no measure of its size was kept: neither the number of refused connections nor the share of callers.

Browsers, mobile apps and partner integrations are the clients that validate the certificate, so any of them could be among the refused.

## How it was found

The check that separated this from other certificate faults was a handshake attempt with `openssl s_client` against the pods. It ended in verify code 21, the code RB-025 attaches to an intermediate missing from the served file. A lapsed leaf gives a different code, and a wrong device clock would have shown up as one caller failing, not as trouble across the edge. Code 21 on the pods put the fault in the file the pods serve.

## What the file was

The certificate comes from a public CA and is stored in the Secret `edge-public-tls`, from which each pod mounts it as files. On this day those files held the certificate for our own name and no intermediate beside it. Since all three pods read the same files, the choice of pod could not help a client. Envoy reads the certificate when a pod starts, so a changed Secret reaches a pod only through a restart of the Deployment; team-platform renews the certificate 30 days ahead of expiry.

## Ending

The record holds two clock times, the start at 16:07 and the end at 16:32, and everything in between is undated. team-platform took the incident, and by 16:32 handshakes were succeeding again. The change that ended it was not recorded. RB-025 gives the remedy for a missing intermediate as a single file with our certificate first and the intermediate after it. How the intermediate came to be missing is not recorded.

## Proposals

1. Keep the per-minute handshake and request counts from the listener stats alongside every change to the Secret, so a before and after can be compared directly.
2. Whenever the Secret's files change, list the certificates the new file contains and keep that list with the change.

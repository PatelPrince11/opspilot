# redis-cache-01 lost, 9 December 2025, early morning

sev2, 03:12 to 03:40 UTC, 28 minutes from first error to close. Runbook: RB-017.

Customers did not lose the catalog. They got it slower: for roughly 40 s the cache was unreachable, and for a while after that most product reads went to catalog-db-01 because the new primary started out nearly empty. The timeline:

**03:12** The redis-cache-01 VM is gone. Nothing on it answers and nothing tells the clients, so catalog-api calls to the cache hang and end in `ETIMEDOUT`. catalog-api begins logging `CAT-E301`, meaning it is reading from catalog-db-01 because the cache did not deliver.

**03:12:30** The Sentinels have heard nothing for the configured 30 s and agree redis-cache-01 is down. They elect and promote redis-cache-02.

**03:12:40** Clients have found redis-cache-02. From a client's side the outage was about 40 s: 30 s of waiting for the verdict and 10 s for the promotion and for clients to find the new node. `CAT-E301` stops being logged here.

**03:13** The cache on the new primary holds little. Redis normally absorbs about 95% of product reads, and those now land on catalog-db-01, whose CPU climbs and reaches about 90%.

**03:14** On-call has the incident open. A timeout, not a refusal, fits a VM that vanished. Asking the Sentinel on redis-cache-02 for the current primary gives redis-cache-02, so no manual promotion is needed and none is attempted.

**Also at 03:12** Two other services, payments-api and inventory-service, saw Redis time out as well. Each keeps its reference data in memory, reloaded every 60 s, and kept using it, so their databases got no extra reads. Those errors needed no action.

**Through the morning** The call that mattered was to wait: as popular products get cached again the hit rate rises and database CPU eases. The thing at risk was catalog-db-01, not Redis.

**03:40** catalog-db-01 CPU is back in its normal range and Sentinel points at a primary that answers. Closed.

## Open

- Why the redis-cache-01 VM was lost is not known.
- For the ticket: about 40 s unreachable, database peak near 90% CPU.

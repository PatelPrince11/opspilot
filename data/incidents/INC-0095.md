# #platform-oncall, Monday 27 October 2025: payments and the bouncers

Digest of the thread, sev2. UTC. The trouble started at 11:05 and was gone at 11:40, so 35 minutes. Both payments-pgbouncer-01 and payments-pgbouncer-02 are involved. Runbook cited: RB-011.

**11:05 payments on-call:** start of the incident. `PAYAPI-E1042` is appearing in payments-api, meaning requests gave up waiting for a database connection inside the service. Payment requests are slow and some are failing.

**payments on-call, shortly after:** both bouncers show the same picture, so this is not one bad instance. Clients are queueing inside the bouncers and some are being disconnected. The bouncer log carries `pooler error: query_wait_timeout`.

**dba on-call:** that log line is PgBouncer ending a client that waited more than 10 seconds for a server connection. It points at a queue, not at the client.

**payments on-call:** RB-011 is the page for this. Saturation there means transactions got longer or more numerous, and the clients behind them queue. We are leaving pool sizes alone while we look at the transactions.

**dba on-call:** agreed; the wait is in front of Postgres.

**dba on-call:** the same picture on both bouncers fits payments-api sending its connections to the two of them about evenly, so whatever holds them is upstream of the bouncers, not tied to one of them.

**payments on-call:** long transactions are coming from payments-api through both bouncers. Which code path or job opened them is not known.

**11:40 payments on-call:** queues are empty on both bouncers, `PAYAPI-E1042` has stopped and payments look normal. Closing. The action that ended the long transactions was not recorded.

**Follow-ups agreed in the thread**

- Work out from the database statistics which transactions were open that long, if they still allow it.
- Put a one-line explanation of `query_wait_timeout` into the paging text, since the line is easy to misread.
- Give payments-api a lower ceiling on how long any single transaction may stay open.

# Refunds export starved the payments bouncers (sev2)

**When:** 2025-06-24, 13:20 to 14:05 UTC · **Owner:** team-payments · **Runbooks used:** RB-011, RB-010

## Summary

For 45 minutes on a Tuesday afternoon, card payments slowed down and a growing share of them failed with `503`. payments-api logged `PAYAPI-E1042` on all four pods. Nothing had crashed. A refunds export that finance ops had asked for was running inside payments-api itself, and its long transactions occupied the database connections that live payment traffic needed. The queue for those connections formed inside PgBouncer, on both instances at once.

## Impact

- `/v1/payments/*` latency started rising around 13:20. Failures followed within minutes and were worst around 13:40.
- A request that could not get a pool connection inside payments-api's 500 ms acquire deadline returned `503`. Customers saw a failed payment and could try again.
- No payment was recorded twice and nothing was lost. Postgres itself had connections to spare throughout.

## How it looked at first

The page at 13:24 was on the `503` rate for payment routes. The pool panels told the familiar story from RB-010: 40 of 40 connections acquired on every pod, acquire wait p99 pinned at the deadline. RB-010 then asks what the database sessions are doing. They were `active`, not idle inside an open transaction, so the pods were not sitting on transactions while calling something else. They were waiting in line.

## Investigation

- 13:31 · `SHOW POOLS` on payments-pgbouncer-01 and payments-pgbouncer-02. Both the same: `sv_active` at 50 (40 regular plus 10 reserve), no idle server connections, `cl_waiting` near 30, `maxwait` near 4 s.
- 13:34 · `SHOW CLIENTS` counted about 80 payments-api clients per instance. An even split, so neither bouncer was carrying the other's share.
- 13:36 · the pgbouncer process on payments-pgbouncer-02 was far below a full core. The bouncer was not the bottleneck.
- 13:41 · on payments-db, `pg_stat_activity` grouped by statement: one statement held about 90 sessions with transactions averaging about 2 s. It was the export's.
- 13:44 · about 100 client backends out of 200 allowed by `max_connections`. The database had headroom; the wait lived entirely in the bouncers.

## Why the numbers fit together

Each of the four pods may hold 40 connections (`pool_max_conns=40`), so up to 160 client-side connections, roughly 80 landing on each bouncer. A bouncer opens at most 50 server connections. With all 50 busy, about 80 − 50 = 30 clients are left waiting, which is what `cl_waiting` showed. Two bouncers open at most 2 × 50 = 100 server connections, which matches the backend count.

Waits hovered around 4 s. That is past the 3 s after which the reserve pool opens, and well short of the 10 s after which PgBouncer disconnects a waiting client. So no client was dropped by the bouncer; every failure was payments-api giving up at its own deadline.

A transaction-pooled bouncer lends a server connection to a client for one transaction and takes it back at commit. Payment transactions last milliseconds. The export's took about 2 s each and were issued back to back from all four pods, enough to keep almost all server connections on both bouncers occupied.

## Root cause

The export started at 13:10. It used the same pgx pool, the same database role and the same transaction-mode bouncers as live payments, and nothing capped how many export transactions could be in flight.

## Resolution

- 13:50 · export switched off.
- 13:51 · remaining export statements cancelled with `pg_cancel_backend`. `cl_waiting` dropped to 0 on both bouncers.
- 13:52 · `PAYAPI-E1042` stopped.
- 14:05 · resolved after 13 quiet minutes.

We followed RB-011 and did not raise `pool_max_conns` or `default_pool_size`: more clients would only have lengthened that queue.

## Follow-ups

1. Run exports outside the payment request path, with their own small pool. Owner: team-payments.
2. Alert when `cl_waiting` stays above 0 on either bouncer for a minute, so the page names the queue instead of the `503`s.
3. Exports that finance needs during the day get a size and concurrency review first.

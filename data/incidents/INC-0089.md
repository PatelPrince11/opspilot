# Postmortem: the broker that ran out of root disk, not data disk

**Date:** Saturday 11 October 2025
**Window:** 11:55 to 13:40 UTC (105 minutes)
**Severity:** sev3
**Host:** kafka-01.prod.internal
**Runbooks used:** RB-009, then RB-008
**Written by:** team-platform

## Where we started

At 11:55 the log agent on kafka-01 began sending `ENOSPC` errors about its own bookkeeping files. Shortly after, the broker's files under `/var/log/kafka` stopped getting new lines. Our first reaction was the obvious one for a Kafka host: a broker that is out of space must be out of space for its partitions. That instinct shaped the next 40 minutes.

## Ruling out the data volume

We opened RB-009 and did its first step on every broker: `df -h /data/kafka`. All three looked ordinary and close to each other, which is what replicated data should look like. kafka-01 was not ahead of its peers. If the partition volume had been the problem we would also have expected the broker to stop and producers to start complaining; neither had happened. The broker was still leading and following partitions as usual, and while we worked we saw no under-replication alert.

The `df` numbers settled it quickly, but we did not trust them at first and went on to RB-009's second step, summing partition sizes per topic from `kafka-log-dirs.sh`, to see whether kafka-01 held anything unusual. It did not. Only then did we accept that the data disk was fine. That told us the space problem was on the other filesystem, the root disk, where `/var` lives on our VMs. We stopped the retention work before it began. We did not shorten any topic, which matters because RB-009 is explicit that cutting retention below a consumer's lag throws away unread messages.

## Finding the real consumer of space

Switching to RB-008, `df -h /var` showed the root filesystem completely full. We sized the directories under `/var` and found two candidates worth checking:

- `/var/log/kafka`, where the broker's GC logs are written. Rotation there was working; the directory was a modest size.
- `/var/log/journal`, which was by far the largest.

`journalctl --disk-usage` matched what `du` said. The journald configuration on kafka-01 had no size limit at all. Whatever the broker and the OS had logged since the VM was built was still on disk.

## What we changed

1. 12:41 · `sudo journalctl --vacuum-size=500M` on kafka-01. The root disk dropped back to a comfortable level within seconds.
2. 12:47 · the log agent's `ENOSPC` errors stopped and broker lines in `/var/log/kafka` started updating again.
3. 12:58 · added `SystemMaxUse=500M` to `/etc/systemd/journald.conf` and restarted `systemd-journald`. The disk-usage report afterwards sat under the new cap.
4. 13:40 · after watching usage stay level, we closed the incident.

We did not restart the broker, and we did not touch anything under `/data/kafka`.

## Effect on users

We saw no producer or consumer errors during the window. The broker's data path is on its own volume, so partition writes carried on. What we did lose is local logging on kafka-01 between 11:55 and 12:47: lines the broker tried to write to `/var/log/kafka` in that window may be incomplete, and journal entries from that period could not be stored. If anyone needs broker logs for that stretch, they will have gaps.

## Why it took us a while

About 40 minutes went on the data-volume hypothesis: from 11:55 until 12:35, when we moved on to the root disk. Looking back, the clues were there early. The `ENOSPC` came from the log agent, not from Kafka, and the broker was still healthy. A full `/data/kafka` looks very different. Checking RB-009's first step was cheap and worth doing. Going on to per-topic sizes after `df` had already cleared the volume is where the time went; `df -h /var` in parallel would have pointed at the root disk much sooner.

## What went well

- Nobody deleted files by hand in either `/data/kafka` or `/var/log/journal`.
- No topic retention was changed on the strength of a wrong guess.

## Proposal

Add a line to the on-call notes: on a broker, an `ENOSPC` that comes from the log agent points at `/var`, not at the partition volume.

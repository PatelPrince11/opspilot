# Tuesday afternoon, fraud-scorer-01, and a CPU graph that lied

I was the team-risk on-call on Tuesday 18 March 2025. This sev3 lasted from 15:10 to 16:05 UTC, and this is how it went from my side.

The first thing I saw was not ours. At 15:14 the payments channel flagged a jump in `PAYAPI-W0310` on payments-api-03.prod.internal: about 40 a second, each one a payment where the fraud score arrived too late and payments-api went ahead with its static rules. Those payments were still decided, by the static rules, but the model was being skipped for a lot of traffic, and that is our problem to fix.

So I opened fraud-scorer. Latency on fraud-scorer-01 had a long, ugly tail. Its CPU, on the other hand, looked relaxed: about 45% of its limit on average over the last quarter hour. My honest first thought was that something downstream of us was slow. I looked at the feature store and found nothing interesting there.

What turned me around was RB-002. A pod that is half idle and still slow is the exact pattern it opens with, so at 15:25 I switched the Grafana "Container CPU" panel to its throttling view. fraud-scorer-01 was at 30% of periods throttled. That is above the roughly 25% that we treat as significant. To be sure the panel was not misleading me, I read `cpu.stat` inside the container twice, a few seconds apart, and `nr_throttled` was climbing right along with `nr_periods`.

The average and the throttling are both true at the same time. The quota is handed out per 100 ms period. When requests arrive in clumps, the pod spends its whole allowance in the first part of a busy period and then waits, frozen, for the next one; quiet periods pull the average back down. Requests that land in a frozen stretch simply wait, and the payments side sees a score that is late.

Why was it spending the allowance so fast? `pgrep -P 1` in the pod listed 16 worker processes. `WEB_CONCURRENCY` was unset in the Deployment, so the entrypoint had counted the cores it could see, all 16 of the node, and started a worker for each. The pod's CPU limit is smaller than 16 cores, so on a busy burst those workers drain the quota in a fraction of the period.

I had two levers: change the worker count, or give the pod more CPU. Changing how gunicorn is started felt like a code change to make in daylight with the team, not in the middle of an incident. So at 15:40 I raised the CPU limit on the fraud-scorer Deployment and left the CPU request where it was, so the scheduler's picture of the pods did not move. The rollout finished at 15:51.

By 15:55 the throttling view on fraud-scorer-01 was below the 25% line and the `PAYAPI-W0310` lines from payments-api-03 had stopped. I watched it until 16:05 and closed it.

Things I want us to talk about this week:

- Matching the worker count to the limit, instead of letting it follow whatever node the pod lands on.
- A throttling alert on fraud-scorer, since the average CPU graph alone hid this one.
- Whether the new limit is the right long-term size or just the quickest fix.

## Notes

**Order of checks.** A Kafka host has two disks that can fill, and they call for opposite responses. Partition storage under `/data/kafka` is handled with retention changes, which can discard messages a lagging consumer still needs. The root disk is handled with ordinary housekeeping. Running RB-009's opening `df` across the brokers was quick and told us which world we were in. kafka-03 sat level with its two peers, so retention stayed exactly as it was.

**Suspects on the root disk.** Two directories could plausibly have been responsible: the GC output directory the broker writes and the systemd journal. The broker's directory was small and rotating. The journal was the giant.

**Remedy.** Vacuuming released space at once. The new `SystemMaxUse=500M` line is what stops the journal climbing back.

**Who felt it.** No client errors were seen while we worked. Free space stayed above zero the whole time, so local writes kept succeeding; the warnings showed up with room to spare.

**Suggestion.** Ship the journald limit in the base image used to build VMs, so a new broker starts out bounded.

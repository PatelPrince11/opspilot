# Ticket: orders-api-01 and orders-api-03 pools full, ORD-5003

**Opened:** 2026-02-28 07:05 UTC by the orders-api on-call
**Severity:** sev3
**Hosts:** orders-api-01.prod.internal, orders-api-03.prod.internal
**Runbook:** none linked
**State:** closed 08:05 UTC

## Report

Saturday, last day of the month. Requests through orders-api-01 and orders-api-03 started failing at 07:05 with `ORD-5003`. The service reads and writes orders-db through a `pg` Pool, and on these two pods the pool has no connection to give. Checking what is occupying it, and who owns it. The window is 07:05 to 08:05, so the ticket covers one hour. 

## Updates

**orders-api on-call, before the switch-off**
Pool on both pods sits at its limit of 25 (`max`). Callers wait for a free connection and give up, which is where `ORD-5003` comes from. The two pods together can hold 2 x 25 = 50 connections, and all 50 are checked out.

**orders-api on-call, before the switch-off**
The connections are held by order-summary requests, which month end makes far busier than on an ordinary day. Each one keeps its connection checked out until its own query comes back from orders-db, about 30 seconds a request. A pool of 25 hands out connections slowly when each one is gone for half a minute.

**orders-api on-call, before the switch-off**
The endpoint belongs to team-commerce, who are being asked what to do about it. Raising `max` is not being tried: a larger pool would only let more 30-second requests pile in, and it would add load on orders-db.

**07:58, team-commerce**
The order-summary endpoint was switched off.

**08:05, orders-api on-call**
Pools on orders-api-01 and orders-api-03 have drained: free connections again, no new `ORD-5003`. Closing.

## Why a 25-connection pool was enough to cause this

The pool is shared by every route on the pod. A route that holds a connection for milliseconds barely notices the limit; a route that holds one for about 30 seconds can take the whole pool by itself once enough of its requests arrive together. The month-end summary requests did that on orders-api-01 and orders-api-03, and requests on other routes then found no free connection and failed with `ORD-5003`. The summary queries ran on orders-db for their 30 seconds, which is the time each request kept its connection.

## Points worth keeping

- `ORD-5003` is orders-api reporting that a dependency call failed or timed out from its own side. Here the dependency was orders-db, reached through a pool with nothing left to lend.
- Two pods were affected, and each is counted against its own pool of 25; the figure of 50 is the two pools added, not a shared pool.
- The cause is time, not volume alone: one request holding one connection for about 30 seconds, multiplied by a month-end crowd of such requests, against a pool of 25 per pod.
- Why the summary query took about 30 seconds was not part of this ticket and is not recorded.
- When the endpoint comes back is a team-commerce call; the date was not recorded.

## Suggestion

For team-commerce and the orders-api owners: decide before the next month end how many summary requests may be in flight at once, so the pool keeps room for checkout.

# Postmortem: orders-db-01 out of connection slots, 22 November 2025

Rated sev2, with times in UTC. It started at 22:15 and ended at 22:45, 30 minutes later. The database was orders-db-01.prod.internal. The runbook consulted was RB-012.

## Short version

On a Saturday night the orders database stopped accepting new sessions. The service in front of it, orders-api, could not open the connections it needed and answered with `ORD-5003`. The slots were not used up by customer traffic. A batch Job that belongs to the commerce side of orders-api, running in the `commerce` namespace, had been holding open about 222 sessions that did nothing.

## Where the 297 went

orders-db has `max_connections` of 300. Three of those are kept for superusers, which leaves 297 for application roles. Those are the slots RB-012 counts for this database.

orders-api is sized for a small share of them: three pods, each with a `pg` Pool capped at 25, so 3 x 25 = 75 at most. The rest of the arithmetic is the leak: 222 idle sessions plus those 75 come to 297, exactly the usable total. The Job had opened a new connection for every batch it processed and had never closed one, so each finished batch left a session behind in the `idle` state. Because the count is "about 222", the sum is a rounded one.

Once no slot was free, a new connection attempt was refused at login with `53300`, before any query was sent. That is why the symptom looked like a failure to connect, not a slow database. orders-api surfaced the refusals as `ORD-5003`.

## What the team saw, and what it decided

- The first sign was in orders-api: new database connections failed from 22:15.
- The query in RB-012 that lists sessions by role, application and client address is the way to see who holds the slots. The pool arithmetic showed from the outset that orders-api's own pods could account for at most 75, so the other sessions were someone else's.
- The owner of those sessions was the batch Job in `commerce`, and RB-012 asks that the owning team be consulted before idle sessions are ended, so team-commerce was brought in.
- Order of action: the Job was stopped first, so that it would stop adding to the pile; then its idle sessions were ended with `pg_terminate_backend`, after team-commerce agreed.
- The times of those two steps were not recorded. The errors had stopped by 22:45, when the incident was closed.

## Analysis

The server behaved as configured. The gap was upstream of it: a job that opened a connection per batch with no matching close, on a database whose slots are shared with the live order path.

The Job's sessions were idle, so they added no query load of their own. The budget in RB-012 had a line for orders-api but none for a batch client, so counting connections by orders-api pods alone would have looked fine.

## Gaps in the record

- Why the Job never closed its connections was not recorded.
- How long it had been accumulating sessions before 22:15 was not recorded.

## Actions proposed

1. Have the batch Job reuse one connection, or a small pool, and close it when it exits.
2. Add a warning on the count of idle sessions per client address, set well below the usable total.
3. Add the batch Job's connections to the written budget for orders-db, so the 75 is no longer the only number on the page.

# Ten minutes of failed card payments, 5 November 2025

I'm on team-payments, and the sev1 on Wednesday morning was my release. It ran from
09:05 to 09:15 UTC. This is what happened, as I saw it.

I shipped a new card-processor-proxy release that morning. The new pods passed their readiness
checks, so as far as the rollout could tell, everything was fine. It wasn't. From 09:05,
payments that went through the new version of the proxy failed, and payments-api logged
`PAYAPI-E1107` for each one whose attempts were all used up.

The alert on the payment error rate fired at 09:08. The failures began with my release, so
that is where I looked first. At 09:10 I ran
`kubectl -n payments rollout undo deploy/card-processor-proxy` and watched both instances,
card-processor-proxy-01 as well as card-processor-proxy-02, go back to the previous version.
As the old version took over again,
`PAYAPI-E1107` tailed off and then stopped. I watched the error rate stay flat and closed the
incident at 09:15, 10 minutes after the first failure.

Ten minutes is short for a sev1, but each of those minutes meant card payments failing. What kept
it short was having a lever that needed no understanding of the bug: the previous version had
been serving payments without trouble right up to 09:05, and an undo puts it back. I would rather
have that lever ready than try to be clever under pressure.

What I don't know yet is which change in the release broke the calls to the processor. Reading
the diff is today's job; until then the cause stays open.

What I'd change:

1. A pod passing its readiness check proved nothing about calls to the processor. Making a
   pod complete one test call before it counts as ready would have stopped this release before
   it took real payments.
2. A pause in the rollout after the first new instance, long enough to watch `PAYAPI-E1107`,
   would have limited how many payments met the bad version.
3. Proxy releases go out off-peak, with a second person watching the payment error rate.
4. The rollback was the right lever and it was quick. I'd like the undo command in our release
   checklist, so whoever is shipping doesn't have to look it up with failures climbing.

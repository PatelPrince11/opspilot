## Platform on-call

- 15:26: `df -h /var` on the host, 100%. Mentioned that the log agent had been sending `ENOSPC` about its own state file for a while, which is how the problem was visible remotely at all.
- 15:34: the size breakdown of `/var` showed one dominant directory, `/var/log/journal`. Asked the channel whether anyone had changed logging on this VM recently. Nobody had.
- 15:40: `journalctl --disk-usage` agreed. Pointed out that `journald.conf` on this host had no size setting at all.
- 15:47: ran `sudo journalctl --vacuum-size=500M`. Posted the before and after free-space numbers in the thread; `/var` went from full to mostly empty.
- 15:55: added `SystemMaxUse=500M` to `/etc/systemd/journald.conf`, restarted journald, and posted the new `--disk-usage` output, now under the limit.
- 16:37: closed it after watching usage hold for a while. Total 75 minutes.

## inventory-service engineer

- Joined at 15:30 to ask whether reservations were at risk. Their dashboards showed normal error rates and latency throughout, and they did not see `ENOSPC` anywhere in the service's own logs.
- Asked whether other database VMs could be carrying the same gap. Nobody knew; that became the thread's one follow-up (below).

## Things the thread agreed on

- The database was never the problem; the root disk was, and it was full because a log store had no ceiling.
- Vacuuming through journald, rather than deleting journal files by hand, was the right call: journald keeps its own record of its files.

## Follow-up

Survey `journald.conf` across the datastore VMs to find any others without a size limit.

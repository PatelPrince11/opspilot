# Slow processor replies, Saturday 15 March 2025

I took the page for this one, a sev3 that ran from 18:15 to 18:35 UTC, which is 20 minutes. It is a short story, because the cause lay at the processor and little else is known about it.

**How it showed up.** The first signal was the error at the top of the stack: callers of payments-api were being handed `504` on card payments. A gateway timeout says something waited too long, so I looked along the path for the slow hop. payments-api was waiting on card-processor-proxy, and the proxy was waiting on the processor.

**Where the delay was.** Call times to the processor were long on the first egress host and on the second, and both began in the same window. When two separate instances show the same delay together, the delay is unlikely to belong to either of them, and it pointed at the far side of the proxy.

**What the callers got.** A card payment sent to payments-api in those 20 minutes could come back as a gateway timeout instead of an approval or a decline. The payment path is short: payments-api, one of the two proxies, then the processor. The slow step in it was the last one, so the callers above it waited behind that step, and payments-api reported the wait as a timeout.

**What is not known.** Why the processor slowed down is not known. We hold no explanation from the processor, and the request rate toward it during the window was not recorded, so whether our own traffic played a part cannot be said.

**Decisions.** Nothing on our side looked worth restarting or scaling: restarting the proxies would not make the processor reply faster, and neither would more capacity. I watched the call durations and kept the channel informed.

**Outcome.** By 18:35 calls were returning at their usual speed and the `504` responses had stopped.

**Afterwards.** No runbook covers a slow processor. A per-minute record of call duration at the proxy would let the start and end of a spell like this be read from a chart.

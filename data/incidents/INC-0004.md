# Card payments failed from midnight on an expired processor certificate, 2025-01-14

payments timeline, sev1, 00:00 to 02:40 UTC.

```
00:00  the certificate that card-processor-proxy presents to the processor passes its end
       date. card-processor-proxy-01 and card-processor-proxy-02 both start logging CPX-E600
       on every outbound call
00:00  payments-api spends 3 attempts on each card payment, then logs PAYAPI-E1107
00:04  payments on-call paged
00:25  the certificate held in the Secret is inspected; its end date is midnight, which
       matches the first error
00:40  request for a replacement certificate goes to the processor
02:30  the replacement arrives
02:38  Secret updated with the new certificate and both proxies restarted
02:40  CPX-E600 stops on both proxies; card payments succeed again
```

## What the first hour looked like

The change was abrupt. The certificate worked until its end date and failed from the next
second, so there was no slow build-up to read and no lead-in on any dashboard. Card payments
were failing from 00:00, and the page arrived four minutes later at 00:04.

Because it was the small hours, traffic was light, yet each pod still logged around 3.5
failures every second. payments-api gave up on a payment after every three of them.

## Waiting on the processor

The certificate comes from the processor, not from our own CA, so nothing could be minted
on our side. Once the end date was confirmed at 00:25, the work was the request at 00:40 and
then the wait until 02:30. The proxy loads the certificate only when it starts, so the
Secret update at 02:38 went together with the restart; the errors were gone two minutes
after that.

## Scope

Both proxies were affected from the same minute, so this was not one bad pod and a restart of
either on its own would have changed nothing. The failure was in the credential both of them
share, and it stayed in place until the replacement was loaded on each.
